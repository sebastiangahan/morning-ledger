Brampton plant showdown day: Ford warns Ottawa off Chinese car kits as all sides meet Stellantis

Today’s meeting is meant to be about laid-off workers and broken promises. A letter from the premier has added China to the agenda.

What happenedFederal Industry Minister Mélanie Joly meets today with Stellantis, the Ontario government and Unifor, the union for the plant’s roughly 2,200 laid-off workers. The meeting follows the company’s Monday announcement that it will sell its idle Brampton assembly plant to Roshel, a Canadian maker of armoured vehicles. Ahead of it, Ontario Premier Doug Ford wrote to Prime Minister Mark Carney saying Chinese electric-vehicle kits must not be assembled at the plant, CTV reports. Separately, Joly’s office told CP24 that she “didn’t meet with Roshel before [the] deal [was] signed with Stellantis.”

The backgroundStellantis owns Jeep, Dodge and Chrysler. In 2023 the federal and Ontario governments promised it up to $15 billion over 10 years, mainly for an electric-vehicle battery factory in Windsor, and the company agreed to keep its Ontario plants, Brampton included, running through 2035. Brampton has built nothing since December 2023, and the Jeep it was meant to make moved to Illinois after U.S. tariffs. Ford leads Ontario’s Progressive Conservatives; Carney leads the federal Liberals. A “kit” is a car shipped in parts and bolted together locally, a common way for foreign automakers to get around tariffs on finished vehicles. Canada agreed in January to let in a limited number of Chinese-made EVs at a lower tariff, and Carney heads to China next week.

How it is being framed

CTV, CP24 (centre). Lead with Ford’s letter and with Joly’s office distancing her from the sale, after CTV reported, citing unnamed sources, that Ottawa had been closely involved in the talks.

Global News (centre). Calls today a “high-stakes meeting” and repeats Unifor’s view that the deal is “underhanded” and Carney’s that it is “a betrayal.”

Right-leaning outlets. Earlier this week the Toronto Sun and National Post led with Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre’s demand that Ottawa sue. I did not see new right-leaning coverage this morning.

Unverified. I have not seen the text of Ford’s letter, only CTV’s headline on it. Whether any Chinese automaker has actually shown interest in Brampton is not reported.

Why it matters to you

EconomyBrampton once supported 10,000 to 12,000 supplier jobs across the Toronto region, according to the auto-parts industry. Armoured vehicles are a far smaller business.

Foreign relationsOntario’s premier is publicly drawing a line on China days before the Prime Minister meets Xi Jinping. Trump has also threatened a 50% tariff on Canadian autos from Jan. 1.

Democracy and ideology lensTwo questions sit under this fight. One is accountability: if a company takes public money and walks away, can governments enforce the deal? The other is economic security: whether Canada should welcome Chinese manufacturing to fill empty plants, or keep it out as a security and trade risk, a debate that now splits people within parties as much as between them.

ConfirmedForeign policy

Carney lands in Dublin as Ottawa reimposes sanctions on Iran

What happenedPrime Minister Mark Carney arrived in Dublin and is set to address European leaders, the first stop on a trip that continues to the Philippines, Cambodia and the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China. As he left, the government restored sanctions on Iran that Canada lifted about a decade ago, CTV reports. CTV also reports that Canadian exports to China surged in the run-up to the visit; it gave no figures in what I saw.

The backgroundSanctions are legal limits on trade, money transfers and travel aimed at a country or specific people. Canada eased some Iran sanctions around 2016, when Western countries struck a deal with Tehran to limit its nuclear program, and has been tightening them since. Canada cut diplomatic ties with Iran in 2012 and listed its Revolutionary Guard as a terrorist group in 2024. This year the United States and Israel have been at war with Iran, which pushed oil prices up. The Europe leg matters because Carney’s economic plan depends on selling more to customers other than the U.S., which buys about three-quarters of Canada’s exports.

How it is being framed

CTV, CP24 (centre). Straight trip coverage: Carney in Dublin, Europe first, then Asia.

Global News (centre). Frames the trip around the Remembrance Day send-off and the Stellantis fight he leaves behind.

Not found. I did not see the specific Iran measures or any opposition response this morning. Treat the scope of the sanctions as unconfirmed until the government publishes details.

Why it matters to you

Foreign relationsMoving on Iran just before meeting Europeans, and before a China visit, shows Canada lining up with allies on security while it shops for trade.

EconomyEurope already has a free-trade deal with Canada (CETA). More use of it is the quickest way to reduce reliance on the U.S.

Democracy and ideology lensSanctions are how democracies punish governments without going to war. Supporters say they signal values and limit a regime’s resources. Sceptics across the spectrum note they often hurt ordinary people more than leaders. Iran’s government has crushed protests and holds dual nationals, including Canadians, which keeps the issue personal here.

ConfirmedPublic money

Ottawa wrote off $4.5 billion in COVID benefits it was owed, the auditor general says

What happenedThe federal government wrote off $4.5 billion in COVID-19 benefit repayments in 2025, according to the auditor general, Global News reports. A write-off means the government formally stops trying to collect money it is owed.

The backgroundThe auditor general is Parliament’s independent watchdog on spending, reporting to MPs rather than to the government. During the pandemic Ottawa paid emergency benefits such as CERB, the Canada Emergency Response Benefit, quickly and on an honour system, then checked eligibility later. Millions of Canadians were later told to pay some or all of it back. Earlier audits found billions went to people who may not have qualified. Collecting small debts from many people costs money and can hit low-income households hardest, which is the government’s usual case for writing some off.

How it is being framed

Global News (centre). A straight report of the audit finding.

Likely lines. Conservatives have long called pandemic spending wasteful, and anti-poverty groups have asked Ottawa to forgive repayments for low-income people. I did not see either side’s response to this report this morning, so treat this as context, not quotes.

Not confirmed. I have not read the audit itself, so how much was ineligible payments versus genuine hardship cases is not yet clear.

Why it matters to you

Economy$4.5 billion is roughly what Ottawa spends on several mid-sized programs in a year. The federal budget delivered last week already runs a large deficit.

PolicyThe lesson for the next emergency is whether to pay fast and check later, or check first and pay slower.

Democracy and ideology lensIndependent auditors are part of how a democracy keeps track of its own spending. The ideological split is familiar: fiscal conservatives see a failure of controls, while the centre-left argues speed saved jobs and lives and some losses were the price of it.

Sources: Global News

The legislature returns Monday, and Toronto’s money question is still unanswered

What happenedOntario’s Finance Minister, Peter Bethlenfalvy, says the province is not reopening talks on a new funding deal for Toronto, despite Mayor Olivia Chow’s push, Global News reports. Premier Doug Ford said last week there is “no 2.0.” Members of the provincial parliament (MPPs) return to Queen’s Park on Monday.

The backgroundToronto relies mostly on property tax. In 2023 the province gave the city $1.2 billion in operating money and took over the Gardiner Expressway and Don Valley Parkway. That deal ends this year, and the province cites a $16.9-billion deficit. Chow, who comes from the NDP and governs from the left, was re-elected on Oct. 26 promising tax increases near inflation. For a sense of the alternative, Calgary’s proposed budget includes a 15% property-tax increase in 2027, which could add up to 37% over four years, Global reports.

How it is being framed

Province. Toronto has received about $9 billion with federal help and must manage its own budget.

City and opposition. Without a new deal the city faces higher taxes or service cuts. The Ontario NDP and Liberals plan to press the government when the house returns.

Also on the agenda. CP24 reports a patient with stage 4 cancer urging the province to fix lab-technologist shortages that are delaying test results.

Why it matters to you

Daily lifeToronto’s 2027 budget, written this winter, decides your property tax (or your landlord’s), TTC service and road repairs.

PolicyThe legislature’s fall sitting is the opposition’s main chance to question the government before the new year.

Democracy and ideology lensCities have no constitutional standing in Canada; provinces can change their powers and funding at will. Fiscal conservatives say tight budgets force discipline. Progressives say cities carry costs like housing and homelessness without the taxes to pay for them.

The lame-duck Congress
DevelopingCongress

Democrats flip Mississippi, and the push to impeach Hegseth stalls

What happenedDemocrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, CBS News reports, calling it an “upset” and Democrats’ eighth Senate pickup. On Monday fewer than 2,500 votes separated them, per the Associated Press. Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune as their leader. In the House, a resolution by Republican Rep. Thomas Massie to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war and operations in Yemen and Venezuela was shelved, and Massie says he will not try again. A petition to force a vote on a new Epstein files bill now has enough signatures.

The backgroundDemocrats won both houses of Congress in last week’s midterm elections, but the winners take their seats on Jan. 3. Until then, the outgoing Republican-led Congress sits as a “lame duck.” Impeachment is the House’s power to charge an official with “high crimes and misdemeanours”; the Senate then holds a trial. A discharge petition lets a majority of House members force a vote leadership does not want. Jeffrey Epstein was a financier and convicted sex offender who died in jail in 2019; demands to release government files on him have split Republicans.

How it is being framed

CBS News (centre-left). Stresses the scale of Republican losses: an “upset” in deep-red Mississippi and Thune re-elected after “brutal midterm losses.”

Fox News (right). Hegseth “thwarts GOP-led impeachment push” while “questions swirl” over whether the charges amount to high crimes. Fox also highlights a memo from new chief of staff Stephen Miller that it says “unleashes” federal agencies.

AP (wire). Speaker Mike Johnson: “I’m still in charge here until early January.” Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries calls the result “a clear mandate.” A defeated Republican, Rep. Richard Hudson, said voters felt “gas was too high.”

Why it matters to Canada

Foreign relationsA larger Democratic Senate makes trade relief more likely to be debated, but tariffs remain the president’s call.

EconomyU.S. funding expires Dec. 11. A shutdown would slow border processing for Canadian trucks and travellers.

Democracy and ideology lensA Republican trying to impeach a Republican cabinet member shows the war has split the president’s own party. Watch the transfer of power: a defeated majority can still pass laws for seven weeks, and the incoming one is promising investigations.

Sources: CBS News · Fox News · AP via AJC

U.S. charges Iran-linked plotters as Tehran targets a top Trump official

What happenedU.S. prosecutors indicted three Iran-linked operatives over alleged terror plots in the United States, Europe and Britain, CBS reports. CBS also reports that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is the latest U.S. official threatened or targeted by Iran. Canada has reimposed Iran sanctions (see Canada). Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, was quoted near US$94.60 on Monday.

The backgroundThe United States and Israel have been striking Iran this year, aiming at its nuclear and missile programs. Iran borders the Strait of Hormuz, the channel that carries about a fifth of the world’s oil. President Trump said this week further strikes are “quite unlikely” and the war is ending “fairly soon”; Iranian officials deny that talks exist. Iran has been accused of plotting against critics and officials abroad for years, including in Canada.

What is disputed

Whether a peace process is real. Washington says talks through Qatar are under way; Tehran calls them “fake news.” An indictment is an accusation, not a conviction, and Iran routinely denies such plots.

Why it matters to you

Daily lifeOil is why gas prices are high and why the Bank of Canada raised its rate in October, which makes variable mortgages and renewals cost more.

EconomyHigh oil lifts Alberta’s revenues while squeezing Ontario households and manufacturers.

Democracy and ideology lensOverseas plots against officials and dissidents are a form of transnational repression: a government reaching across borders to intimidate. The policy debate in Washington is between pressure to force a deal and fear of a longer war that voters just punished at the polls.

Sources: CBS News · TMGM (oil, loonie) · CTV News

EconomyNvidia is a large holding in many index and pension funds, including Canadian ones.

Canada signs an AI safety pledge critics say has no teeth

Disclosure: this newsletter is produced with help from Claude, an Anthropic product. Anthropic is among the pledge’s signatories.

What happenedCanada joined an international AI safety declaration alongside companies including Toronto’s Cohere, Anthropic and OpenAI. Global News describes it as a declaration “without ‘teeth’”: it sets shared principles but no binding rules or penalties.

The backgroundOttawa plans to invest up to $1.6 billion in Cohere as part of what it calls “sovereign AI,” keeping key AI built in Canada. Voluntary pledges are the main form of AI governance so far; Canada’s own AI law died when Parliament was dissolved in 2025.

The debateSupporters say voluntary deals move faster than laws. Critics say they let companies set their own homework.
